## Fig. 1. Exhaustive versus combinatorial designs.
(*A*) An exhaustive pairwise screen of 10 molecules measures the control, each molecule alone, and each of the 45 pairs (56 conditions; rows, conditions; columns, molecules; green, present). (*B*) A combinatorial design of 16 mixtures for the same 10 molecules. Each molecule is present in half of the mixtures, and every pair of molecules occurs in all four presence/absence states; the cells outlined in orange show the mixtures in which molecules 2 and 6 are both present. Because every mixture informs every molecule and every pair, the design is both smaller and statistically more efficient. (*C*) Number of conditions needed as the number of molecules *K* grows: all 2^*K*^ mixtures (dotted), all pairs and triplets (dashed), all pairs (solid gray), and the combinatorial design needed to recover 95% of five pairwise interactions of size 1 with 3 replicates (green circles; open squares, three-way interactions, *K* ≤ 30). Numbers give the fold reduction relative to the exhaustive pairwise screen.

## Fig. 2. Simulations with up to 100 molecules.
(*A*) Fraction of five planted pairwise interactions (size *d* = 1) recovered as a function of the number of mixtures measured, for 18, 50, and 100 molecules. The dotted line marks 95% recall. (*B*) Number of mixtures needed for 95% recall of five interactions of size 0.5, 1, or 2, compared with the size of the exhaustive pairwise screen (gray). (*C*) Fold reduction in the number of conditions relative to the exhaustive pairwise screen, for 1 to 10 interactions of size 1. Values below 1 (*K* = 10) indicate that the exhaustive screen is smaller. (*D*) Number of mixtures needed to recover 95% of one to five three-way interactions of size 1, compared with an exhaustive screen of all pairs and triplets (dashed). (*E*) With the same replication (3 per condition) and the same analysis, the exhaustive pairwise screen (gray) recovers few interactions of size 0.5 or 1, whereas the combinatorial design reaches 95% with far fewer mixtures. Numbers above bars give the number of conditions measured. In all panels, half of the molecules have an effect of their own, a diffuse background of very small interactions is present (total variance 0.1), and results are averages over 30 simulated outputs per point.

## Fig. 3. Benchmark on three real complete factorial experiments.
For each dataset (columns), every one of the 32 mixtures of five factors had been measured, which defines the reference answer. Subsets of 6 to 32 mixtures were analyzed following a combinatorial design (green), a balanced random design (orange), or a one-at-a-time ladder (control, single factors, then pairs, and so on; red). The top axis gives the fraction of the complete design. (*A*) Fraction of the reference single-factor effects recovered. (*B*) Error in predicting the mixtures that were not measured, relative to the noise of a measured mixture mean (dotted line, 1). (*C*) Fraction of the reference interactions (pairs and triplets) recovered. Curves are averages over 30 random draws (transcriptome, bacterial growth) or 20 independent splits of the plants × 5 draws (root traits). Transcriptome, *Arabidopsis* roots under nitrate, ammonium, auxin, cytokinin, and abscisic acid (15,348 genes) [@ristova2016]; root traits, *Arabidopsis* under nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, sulfur, and light (15 traits) [@kellermeier2014]; bacterial growth, *E. coli* under five-antibiotic panels (2,000 panels) [@tekin2018].

## Fig. 4. Interactions among three or more molecules are rare or weak.
(*A*) Share of the variation among the true mixture means explained by single-factor effects and by interactions of each order, corrected for measurement noise and pooled over responsive outputs. (*B*) Same quantity divided by the number of possible terms of each order, which gives the average weight of an individual term (log scale). Values for high orders are upper bounds. (*C*) Percentage of responsive outputs with at least one detectable term of each order in the complete design (false discovery rate 1%). Transcriptome (5 signals), 2,612 responsive genes; transcriptome (3 nutrients), 4,996 genes; root traits, 15 traits; bacterial growth, 10,780 panels. (*D*) Recovery of pairwise (solid) and three-way (dashed) interactions by the combinatorial design in the benchmark of Fig. 3 (colors as in *A*–*C*). The transcriptome contains only five reference three-way interactions.

## Fig. 5. Calibrating the planning simulations on real data.
(*A*) Interaction recovery predicted by a naive simulation (significant effects of the complete design plus Gaussian noise) versus recovery observed on real data, for each dataset, design strategy, number of mixtures, and order (green, pairs; red, triplets). Points below the diagonal indicate optimism. (*B*) Same comparison for the calibrated simulation, in which effects of every order are drawn from the empirical distribution (zero, small, or large) estimated from each dataset. (*C*) Number of mixtures needed to recover 95% of five interactions as a function of their size, without background (solid) or with a dense background of small interactions as in the phenotypic datasets (dashed), for 18, 50, and 100 molecules. Missing points did not reach 95% within the range simulated.

## Fig. S1. Experiments in waves.
The combinatorial sequence was measured in waves of 10, 16, 24, and 32 mixtures; after each wave the model predicted the next wave, and the experiment stopped when the prediction error was within 1.5 times the noise. (*A*) Fraction of outputs for which the complete answer was recovered, with the wave rule (green) or with all 32 mixtures (gray). (*B*) Average number of mixtures used. (*C*) Percentage of outputs for which the rule stopped early with an incomplete answer.

## Fig. S2. Forward selection versus lasso.
Fraction of outputs for which the exact set of reference interactions was recovered by the forward-selection analysis used throughout (green) or by a lasso with all pairs and triplets as candidates and the penalty chosen by the Bayesian information criterion (orange), for the combinatorial design. (*A*) Plant transcriptome (400 outputs with effects). (*B*) Bacterial growth (400 panels).
